Technique of the week · Mastering delivery

The Pause
Silence is part of the speech

A two-second pause reads as confidence; an 'um' reads as doubt.

The idea

Pauses give the audience time to absorb a point, and give you time to breathe and think. Nervous speakers fill silence because it feels endless from the inside. From the outside, it feels like authority.

How to do it

  1. Mark the three most important sentences in your talk. Pause after each one.
  2. Before you start, stand, look at the room, and count two in silence.
  3. When you feel a filler word coming, close your mouth instead.
In the session

The drill

The um-buzzer: one-minute talks where the group taps the table for every filler. Then repeat the same talk, replacing each one with a pause.

This week

Your homework

In one conversation today, let a silence sit for two seconds before you answer.

When you give feedback, ask

Where did a pause make a point land? Where did they rush?

Source: Delivery (actio/pronuntiatio) in Quintilian, Institutio Oratoria XI.3.
