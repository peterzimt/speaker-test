Pitching Yourself
Who you are, in sixty seconds

Not your job title — the problem you solve and the proof you've solved it.

The idea

Interviews, networking, the round of introductions at a new job: you'll describe yourself hundreds of times. A good self-pitch has a clear role, one vivid proof and a hook for the next question.

How to do it

  1. I help [who] with [what problem].
  2. One proof: a specific result or story, with a number if you have one.
  3. End with what you're looking for, so the other person knows how to help.
In the session

The drill

Speed-pitching: pairs, 60 seconds each, rotate three times. Refine the pitch after every round based on the questions you got.

This week

Your homework

Write your pitch on a card and say it out loud three times before your next event.

When you give feedback, ask

After 60 seconds, could you introduce them to someone else?

Source: Ethos applied to self-presentation; Schlenker & Leary (1982).
