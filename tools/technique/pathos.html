Technique of the week · Connecting with your audience

Pathos
Make them feel it, not just know it

People act on what they feel. Give your point a face, a moment and a stake.

The idea

Pathos is persuasion through the emotions you raise in the audience. Not manipulation: it's showing why the point matters to real people. The fastest route is the specific — one person, one moment, one detail — rather than big abstract words.

How to do it

  1. Swap a statistic for a single person it happened to.
  2. Zoom in on one sensory detail: a sound, an object, a line someone said.
  3. Say what's at stake for the people in this room, now.
In the session

The drill

Take a dry fact ("30% of projects run late") and turn it into a 45-second story about one person. Same fact, felt.

This week

Your homework

When you explain something today, add 'for example, last week…' and tell the specific case.

When you give feedback, ask

Which moment did you feel something? Was it earned, or pushed?

Source: Aristotle, Rhetoric, Book II, ch. 2–11.
