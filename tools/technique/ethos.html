Technique of the week · Connecting with your audience

Ethos
Why should they listen to you?

Earn the room's trust in the first thirty seconds — before you ask it to believe anything.

The idea

For Aristotle, ethos is the persuasion that comes from the speaker's character as the audience perceives it: competence, good intentions and goodwill towards them. It isn't your CV. It's the evidence, shown live, that you know what you're talking about and that you're on their side.

How to do it

  1. Name your link to the topic in one concrete sentence — something you did, saw or paid for, not a title.
  2. Show goodwill: say what the audience gets out of the next few minutes.
  3. Admit one limit honestly. A small concession makes the rest more credible.
In the session

The drill

60-second introductions: each person presents a topic they know well, opening with a single ethos line. The group calls out which line made them lean in.

This week

Your homework

In your next meeting, replace 'I think' with the reason you have standing to think it: 'Having run this twice…'.

When you give feedback, ask

Did I believe them before they made their main point? What gave it away?

Source: Aristotle, Rhetoric, Book I, ch. 2 and Book II, ch. 1.
