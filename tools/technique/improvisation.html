Improvisation
Speaking without a script

You don't need more ideas — you need a structure you can grab in three seconds.

The idea

Impromptu speaking is a skill of structure, not inspiration. With one or two reliable frames — Point–Reason–Example–Point, or Past–Present–Future — you can turn any question into a clear minute.

How to do it

  1. Take a breath and say your point first, even if it's simple.
  2. Give one reason and one concrete example.
  3. Repeat your point in different words and stop.
In the session

The drill

The impro wheel: a random topic, a random structure, a random constraint. Thirty seconds to think, ninety to speak.

This week

Your homework

When someone asks your opinion today, answer in PREP: point, reason, example, point.

When you give feedback, ask

Could you see the structure? Did they finish cleanly or trail off?

Source: PREP and similar frames used in debate and Toastmasters Table Topics.
